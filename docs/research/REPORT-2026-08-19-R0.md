## Layout sweep — what is optimal?

Four layouts were run under the *same* synthetic stub, same seeds, so the only change is `hidden` and activation.

| Layout | W | FLOPs/bot | g30 best | g30 mean | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H08 tanh | 165 | 304 | **2.512** | 2.434 | Fastest to converge on the stub |
| H12 tanh | 245 | 456 | 2.488 | 2.417 | Close second |
| **H16 tanh** | **325** | **608** | 2.441 | 2.332 | Chosen — see below |
| H24 tanh | 485 | 912 | 2.436 | 2.290 | More capacity, slower on toy stub |
| H32 tanh | 645 | 1216 | 2.133 | 2.004 | Over-param for the stub's capacity |
| H16 relu | 325 | 608 | 2.347 | 2.203 | Near-identical to tanh on the stub |

Sweep artifacts: `tools/ga/sweeps/sweep_H24_g20_s42.png`, `sweep_H32_g30_s42.png`, `sweep_42.json` (per-gen curves).

### Decision: keep **14→16(tanh)→5** as the shipped shape

H08 wins on the toy stub because the stub's decision surface is small (a few `sigmoid` thresholds). On a real sim with walls, LOS jitter, and 14 input correlations the extra capacity matters — `H16` is only `~300` extra floats (~0.3 KiB) and `~150` extra MACs/bot/tick (`0.05 µs`). The shipped `BotNeuralBrain` is `16 hidden` already, and every doc, `BotConfig`, and `tools/ga` table says `16`. We keep it.

Practical note: the sweep proves the **pipeline discriminates layouts correctly** (it ranked them, plotted on one chart, json-diffable). When the headless harness is live, rerun the same sweep with `sweep.py --hidden 8 16 24 32 --activations tanh relu --pop 32 --gens 80` and let the real sim pick.
